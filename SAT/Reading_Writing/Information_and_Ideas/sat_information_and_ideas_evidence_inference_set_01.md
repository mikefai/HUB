## ❓ Question Items

### Question 1: Central Ideas & Details (Literature / History)
The following text is adapted from Edith Wharton's 1905 novel *The House of Mirth*. Lily Bart is reflecting on her social standing and financial vulnerability in high-society New York.

> Selden had once told her that he considered her the most adaptable creature he had ever met; but she was beginning to see that her adaptability was merely the negative ability to fit herself into any pattern that circumstances imposed. She had been taught to believe that a woman's first duty was to be ornamental, and she had fulfilled that requirement with an effortless grace that concealed the tedious mechanics of the process. Yet, underneath this polished facade, she harbored an unsettling awareness: the very luxuries that sustained her had simultaneously rendered her incapable of surviving outside their narrow, gilded perimeter.

Which choice best states the primary purpose of the text?

- **A)** To argue that Lily Bart possesses an innate desire to rebel against the rigid aristocratic traditions of New York society.
- **B)** To illustrate Lily Bart's growing realization that her refined social conformity has made her critically dependent on the world she inhabits.
- **C)** To contrast Selden's superficial assessment of Lily's adaptability with her proven capacity for genuine self-reliance.
- **D)** To condemn the superficiality of high society for deliberately preventing women from acquiring meaningful vocational skills.

### Question 2: Command of Evidence (Textual)
Ecologist Dr. Elena Vance and colleagues investigated the foraging behaviors of *Bombus terricola* (yellow-banded bumblebees) in fragmented forest ecosystems. Conventional ecological models posit that floral density is the sole primary determinant of foraging frequency. However, Vance hypothesized that microclimatic thermal stability—specifically shelter from high-velocity wind turbulence provided by dense canopy cover—exerts a stronger influence on bumblebee visitation rates in sub-alpine regions than floral density alone.

Which finding, if true, would most directly support Dr. Vance's hypothesis?

- **A)** In sub-alpine clearings with low floral density but substantial windbreaks, *B. terricola* foraging frequencies were significantly higher than in high-density floral meadows exposed to sustained winds.
- **B)** *B. terricola* individuals in high-altitude environments exhibited greater metabolic rates when foraging in colder ambient temperatures regardless of wind speed.
- **C)** Forest fragmentation reduced overall populations of both *B. terricola* and other native pollinator species across all surveyed elevations.
- **D)** Bumblebee visitations to sheltered floral patches increased only when the ambient temperature rose above the seasonal average.

### Question 3: Command of Evidence (Quantitative)
Biochemists examined the enzymatic breakdown of microplastic polymers (polyethylene terephthalate, or PET) by engineered bacterial strains over a 14-day incubation period under controlled laboratory conditions.

| Strain Identifier | Polymer Type | Pre-treatment | Degradation Rate (% / day) | Residual Mass after 14 Days (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Wild-type *I. sakaiensis*** | Crystalline PET | None | 0.8% | 88.8% |
| **Engineered PETase-v1** | Crystalline PET | Thermal Annealing | 2.4% | 66.4% |
| **Engineered PETase-v2** | Crystalline PET | UV Irradiation | 4.1% | 42.6% |
| **Engineered Dual-MHETase** | Semi-crystalline PET | UV Irradiation | 5.9% | 17.4% |

The researchers concluded that subjecting polymers to physical pre-treatment accelerates enzymatic cleavage, but maximizing the degradation efficiency of crystalline PET depends predominantly on optimizing the enzyme's catalytic active site rather than increasing incubation duration alone.

Which choice best describes data from the table that supports the researchers' conclusion?

- **A)** Engineered PETase-v1 with thermal annealing reduced residual mass to 66.4%, whereas wild-type without pre-treatment left 88.8% residual mass.
- **B)** Engineered PETase-v2 achieved a higher daily degradation rate (4.1%/day) on crystalline PET than PETase-v1 (2.4%/day) under physical pre-treatment, leaving 42.6% residual mass.
- **C)** Engineered Dual-MHETase left the lowest residual mass (17.4%) when applied to semi-crystalline PET with UV irradiation.
- **D)** Wild-type *I. sakaiensis* degraded crystalline PET at a rate of 0.8% per day without requiring physical pre-treatment.

### Question 4: Inferences (Scientific Discourse)
Astronomers measuring the spectroscopic signatures of exoplanet HD 209458b detected atmospheric water vapor, sodium, and titanium oxide. Intriguingly, subsequent observations during secondary eclipse events revealed that the upper atmosphere exhibits an unexpected thermal inversion—where temperature increases with altitude rather than decreasing. In planetary atmospheres, a thermal inversion requires an abundant gaseous species capable of absorbing high-energy stellar UV radiation at high altitudes. If an exoplanet's upper atmosphere lacks such high-altitude stellar absorbers, solar energy penetrates deeper, heating lower layers first and producing a standard declining temperature profile.

It can most reasonably be inferred from the text that the atmosphere of HD 209458b:

- **A)** Contains a high-altitude absorber that captures significant stellar UV radiation before it penetrates into the lower atmospheric layers.
- **B)** Has completely lost its lower atmospheric layers due to intense stellar radiation during secondary eclipses.
- **C)** Experiences greater temperature swings in its lower atmosphere than exoplanets without thermal inversions.
- **D)** Is composed entirely of titanium oxide and sodium with negligible traces of hydrogen or helium.

### Question 5: Inferences (Humanities / Social Sciences)
Historical linguists studying the preservation of endangered Celtic languages in Brittany observed that while regional Breton radio broadcasts and bilingual street signage expanded significantly between 1995 and 2020, intergenerational transmission within family households declined from 34% to under 9%. Sociolinguists argue that institutional top-down recognition (such as official signage and media broadcasting) cannot substitute for the organic, daily communicative utility within familial domestic environments that natively anchors living language acquisition.

Based on the text, what would the sociolinguists most likely conclude about language preservation initiatives?

- **A)** Initiatives focused solely on media visibility and official institutional displays are insufficient on their own to halt language obsolescence.
- **B)** Family households in Brittany deliberately reject official bilingual signage because it undermines cultural autonomy.
- **C)** Intergenerational transmission is easier to measure quantitatively than radio broadcasting hours.
- **D)** Celtic languages will completely disappear unless bilingual street signs are eliminated in favor of domestic education programs.

## 💡 Granular Distractor Analysis & Solution Rationales

### Question 1
- **Correct Answer: B**
  - **Textual Evidence**: The text notes that Lily realizes her adaptability was "merely the negative ability to fit herself into any pattern," and that underneath her graceful facade, her luxuries had "simultaneously rendered her incapable of surviving outside their narrow, gilded perimeter." This directly matches Choice B.
- **Distractor Breakdown**:
  - **A (*Innate desire to rebel*)**: *Incorrect.* The passage emphasizes her conformity and adaptation, not an active rebellion against aristocratic norms.
  - **C (*Self-reliance*)**: *Incorrect.* The text states the exact opposite—she is *incapable* of surviving outside her gilded social circle.
  - **D (*Societal conspiracy against vocational skills*)**: *Incorrect.* While society conditioned her to be "ornamental," the text does not discuss vocational skills or an institutional conspiracy.

---

### Question 2
- **Correct Answer: A**
  - **Textual Evidence**: Vance's hypothesis is that wind shelter (microclimatic thermal stability) is a stronger driver of foraging frequency than floral density. Choice A directly tests this by comparing low floral density + high shelter against high floral density + high wind, showing that shelter produced higher foraging frequency.
- **Distractor Breakdown**:
  - **B (*Metabolic rates*)**: *Incorrect.* Metabolic rates in cold air do not address foraging frequency or compare windbreaks with floral density.
  - **C (*Overall population decline*)**: *Incorrect.* General demographic reduction across elevations provides no comparative evidence regarding microclimatic wind stability vs floral density.
  - **D (*Average seasonal temperature threshold*)**: *Incorrect.* This introduces an unrelated variable without contrasting floral density against wind turbulence.

---

### Question 3
- **Correct Answer: B**
  - **Quantitative Evidence**: The conclusion has two prongs: physical pre-treatment helps, but catalytic optimization of the enzyme is predominant on crystalline PET. Choice B compares two pre-treated variants on crystalline PET (PETase-v1 at 2.4% vs PETase-v2 at 4.1% degradation rate), directly demonstrating how optimizing the active site variant drastically accelerates degradation under physical pre-treatment.
- **Distractor Breakdown**:
  - **A (*V1 vs wild-type*)**: *Incorrect.* While true, this only demonstrates the benefit of pre-treatment/engineering broadly, failing to distinguish between specific enzymatic iterations on crystalline PET.
  - **C (*Dual-MHETase on semi-crystalline*)**: *Incorrect.* Dual-MHETase was tested on *semi-crystalline* PET, so it cannot support claims specific to *crystalline* PET.
  - **D (*Wild-type baseline*)**: *Incorrect.* Stating the baseline rate does not substantiate the comparative efficacy of active-site optimization.

---

### Question 4
- **Correct Answer: A**
  - **Logical Proof**: The passage establishes a strict causal rule: thermal inversions (upper layers warmer than lower) require an abundant high-altitude stellar UV absorber. Because HD 209458b exhibits a thermal inversion, it must contain a high-altitude absorber that captures UV radiation before it penetrates downward.
- **Distractor Breakdown**:
  - **B (*Lost lower atmospheric layers*)**: *Incorrect.* The text mentions thermal profiles across atmospheric depths; it does not claim the lower atmosphere has been stripped away.
  - **C (*Greater temperature swings*)**: *Incorrect.* The text discusses the direction of temperature gradients with altitude, not diurnal/orbital temperature volatility.
  - **D (*Composition exclusively Ti-oxide and Na*)**: *Incorrect.* The text explicitly mentions water vapor as well, and "composed entirely" is an unwarranted extreme claim.

---

### Question 5
- **Correct Answer: A**
  - **Logical Proof**: The text contrasts increasing public institutional measures (signs, radio) with collapsing domestic intergenerational transmission (34% down to 9%), and notes the sociolinguists' argument that top-down measures cannot substitute for familial communication. Thus, public/media visibility alone cannot prevent language obsolescence.
- **Distractor Breakdown**:
  - **B (*Families deliberately reject signage*)**: *Incorrect.* The text presents no evidence of active hostility or rejection from families.
  - **C (*Measurement difficulty*)**: *Incorrect.* The text provides concrete percentages for both; ease of measurement is not discussed.
  - **D (*Eliminating bilingual signs*)**: *Incorrect.* The sociolinguists do not advocate removing signs, but rather emphasize that signage alone is insufficient.

- **Correct Answer: D**

#### Question 8
- **Correct Answer: C**
  - **Textual Proof**: Escapes, jar-opening, keeper recognition, and latch-defeating all illustrate intelligent problem-solving.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A**: *Incorrect.* No keeper was harmed; mischief is not danger.
  - **B**: *Incorrect.* Locks are a detail, not the main idea.
  - **D**: *Incorrect.* Keeper kindness is background, not the point.

#### Question 9
- **Correct Answer: D**
  - **Textual Proof**: An 18% drop in downtown car entries the launch year directly evidences fewer car trips.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A**: *Incorrect.* Bike color is irrelevant.
  - **B**: *Incorrect.* One rider proves nothing about trips.
  - **C**: *Incorrect.* "Many unrelated reasons" weakens rather than supports the claim.

#### Question 10
- **Correct Answer: B**
  - **Textual Proof**: Majorities first-time (60%) and under forty (80%) compose a young, new audience.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A**: *Incorrect.* Directly contradicted by 60% first-timers.
  - **C**: *Incorrect.* Nothing about older attitudes was measured.
  - **D**: *Incorrect.* Price was never surveyed.

#### Question 11
- **Correct Answer: C**
  - **Textual Proof**: Competing findings (pesticides vs. mites) with recovery under mite treatment leave the cause genuinely debated.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A**: *Incorrect.* "Sole cause" overstates contested evidence.
  - **B**: *Incorrect.* Mites predict collapse best; they are not ruled out.
  - **D**: *Incorrect.* Decline itself is undisputed in all four studies.

#### Question 12
- **Correct Answer: D**
  - **Textual Proof**: "Quietly, without pay," six years of work, herons returning — the framing admires her.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A**: *Incorrect.* Nothing scornful appears.
  - **B**: *Incorrect.* A full admiring portrait is not indifference.
  - **C**: *Incorrect.* No doubt about her work is raised.
