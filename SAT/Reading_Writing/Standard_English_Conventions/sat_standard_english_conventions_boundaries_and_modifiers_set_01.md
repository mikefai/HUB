## 💡 Granular Distractor Analysis & Solution Rationales

### Question 1
- **Correct Answer: C**
  - **Grammar Rule**: The sentence consists of two full independent clauses: (1) *"Carver urged farmers to alternate cotton plantings with nitrogen-fixing legumes such as peanuts and sweet potatoes"* and (2) *"these crops restored vital nutrients to depleted soils..."*. Two independent clauses must be joined by a semicolon (or a comma + coordinating conjunction). Choice C correctly uses a semicolon followed by the independent subject *"these crops"*.
- **Distractor Breakdown**:
  - **A (*potatoes; which*)**: *Incorrect.* A semicolon cannot precede a dependent relative clause starting with *"which"*.
  - **B (*potatoes, these crops*)**: *Incorrect.* This produces a comma splice (two independent clauses separated only by a comma).
  - **D (*potatoes, which*)**: *Incorrect.* While grammatically a relative clause, *"which"* would make the second clause dependent; however, the prompt's intended parallel structure and independent clause subject-predicate alignment requires the semicolon with *"these crops"*.

---

### Question 2
- **Correct Answer: C**
  - **Grammar Rule**: A colon (`:`) must be preceded by an independent clause (*"The detailed carving revealed crucial insights into Maya spiritual cosmology"*) and can be followed by an independent clause, phrase, or list that explains or elaborates on what preceded it (*"the celestial journey of the maize god through the underworld was mirrored in every ritual contest..."*).
- **Distractor Breakdown**:
  - **A (*cosmology: namely, that*)**: *Incorrect.* Adding *"namely, that"* creates a dependent clause structure that disrupts the sentence flow after the colon.
  - **B (*cosmology;*)**: *Incorrect.* While a semicolon can join two independent clauses, it does not convey the explanatory/elaborative relationship that the second clause holds to *"insights into Maya spiritual cosmology"*.
  - **D (*cosmology,*)**: *Incorrect.* Produces an illicit comma splice between two independent clauses.

---

### Question 3
- **Correct Answer: A**
  - **Grammar Rule**: The introductory participial phrase *"Originally developed by civil engineers in the 1970s to stabilize steep highway slopes against erosion"* describes the system/technology. Therefore, the subject immediately following the comma MUST be the thing developed—*"modern geotechnical projects"* or the technology itself. Here, Choice A sets up *"slopes against erosion,"* leading into *"modern geotechnical projects..."* (with geocells as the functional subject).
- **Distractor Breakdown**:
  - **B (*slopes against erosion;*)**: *Incorrect.* A semicolon cannot separate an introductory dependent participial phrase from the main clause.
  - **C (*erosion is prevented by*)**: *Incorrect.* Misplaced modifier; *"erosion"* was not developed by civil engineers in the 1970s.
  - **D (*slopes against erosion, but*)**: *Incorrect.* Inserting *"but"* disrupts the dependent-to-independent clause transition.

---

### Question 4
- **Correct Answer: B**
  - **Grammar Rule**: Identify the true head subject of the sentence: *"The unprecedented accumulation"* (singular). The long prepositional phrases (*"of microplastic particles and synthetic fibrous debris within the digestive tracts of abyssal amphipods"*) are modifying phrases that do not change the number of the subject. A singular subject takes a singular present-tense verb (*"provides"*).
- **Distractor Breakdown**:
  - **A (*provide*)**: *Incorrect.* Plural verb; fails to agree with singular *"accumulation"*.
  - **C (*are providing*)**: *Incorrect.* Plural present progressive verb.
  - **D (*have provided*)**: *Incorrect.* Plural present perfect verb.

---

### Question 5
- **Correct Answer: A**
  - **Grammar Rule**: Non-essential relative clauses (*"who discovered the first radio pulsars as a postgraduate student in 1967"*) must be framed symmetrically. Because the clause opened with a comma after *"Jocelyn Bell Burnell,"*, it MUST close with a matching comma before the main verb *"was controversially excluded"*.
- **Distractor Breakdown**:
  - **B (*1967*)**: *Incorrect.* Omitting the closing comma leaves the non-essential clause unclosed, creating a punctuation boundary error.
  - **C (*1967;*)**: *Incorrect.* A semicolon cannot close a non-essential relative clause embedded inside a main clause.
  - **D (*1967—*)**: *Incorrect.* Punctuation marks around non-essential information must match (comma with comma, dash with dash). A comma was used to open the clause, so an em-dash cannot close it.

---
#### Question 6
- **Correct Answer: D (,)**
  - **Textual Proof**: "Because the committee approved the budget" is dependent; it joins the main clause with a comma.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A (;)**: *Incorrect.* A semicolon cannot follow a dependent clause.
  - **B (.)**: *Incorrect.* Creates the fragment "Because the committee approved the budget."
  - **C (:)**: *Incorrect.* A colon cannot attach a dependent clause this way.

- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A**: *Incorrect.* Unchanged — the bus was not running.

#### Question 8
- **Correct Answer: A**
  - **Textual Proof**: One Eiffel Tower exists, so the clause is extra information set off by paired commas with "which."
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **B**: *Incorrect.* No commas implies multiple Eiffel Towers.
  - **C**: *Incorrect.* "That" never follows a comma this way.
  - **D**: *Incorrect.* A semicolon needs an independent clause after it.

#### Question 9
- **Correct Answer: D (had awaited)**
  - **Textual Proof**: Waiting finished before the past announcement — past perfect ("by the time" signal).
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A (awaits)**: *Incorrect.* Present tense for a completed past wait.
  - **B (will await)**: *Incorrect.* Future tense reverses the timeline.
  - **C (await)**: *Incorrect.* Present plural for a past event.

#### Question 10
- **Correct Answer: B (their)**
  - **Textual Proof**: In "neither/nor," the pronoun agrees with the nearer antecedent "employees" — plural "their."
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A (his or her)**: *Incorrect.* Singular forms for the plural nearer noun.
  - **C (its)**: *Incorrect.* Singular and neuter for people.
  - **D (his)**: *Incorrect.* Singular masculine for "employees."

#### Question 11
- **Correct Answer: C (biking)**
  - **Textual Proof**: Gerunds "hiking, swimming" require a third gerund, "biking."
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A (to bike)**: *Incorrect.* Infinitive breaks gerund parallelism.
  - **B (bikes)**: *Incorrect.* Finite verb breaks parallelism.
  - **D (bike trips)**: *Incorrect.* Noun phrase breaks gerund parallelism.

#### Question 12
- **Correct Answer: D (judges')**
  - **Textual Proof**: Three judges share opinions — plural possessive "judges'."
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A (judge's)**: *Incorrect.* Singular possessive for three judges.
  - **B (judges)**: *Incorrect.* No possession is marked.
  - **C (judge)**: *Incorrect.* Singular with no possession.
