## 💡 Granular Distractor Analysis & Explanations

### Question 1
- **Correct Answer: B (*stand*)**
  - **Inverted Syntax Rule**: The prepositional phrase *"Surrounding the perimeter of the ancient Persian ceremonial complex of Persepolis"* is the locative predicate. The true subject comes AFTER the verb: *"several imposing limestone colossi"* (plural). A plural subject requires the plural present verb `stand`.
  - **Distractors (A, C, D)**: Singular verbs (*stands, was standing, has stood*) that erroneously agree with *"complex"* or *"Persepolis"*.

### Question 2
- **Correct Answer: B (*their*)**
  - **Antecedent Rule**: The pronoun refers back to *"its blastema cells"* (plural noun). Plural antecedent requires the plural possessive pronoun `their`.
  - **Distractor A (*its*)**: Singular possessive; fails to match plural *"cells"*.
  - **Distractor D (*it's*)**: Contraction for *it is*, which is grammatically impossible before a noun phrase.

### Question 3
- **Correct Answer: A (*subjected*)**
  - **Inverted Third Conditional Rule**: The formal inversion for an unfulfilled past condition is: `Had + Subject + Past Participle (V3)`. With subject *"the aerospace engineers"*, the past participle is `subjected`.
  - **Distractor D (*have subjected*)**: Double auxiliary (*Had ... have subjected*) is ungrammatical.

### Question 4
- **Correct Answer: B (*to penalize*)**
  - **Correlative Parallelism Rule**: Elements joined by `not only [X] but also [Y]` must share identical grammatical form. Since element X is an infinitive phrase (*to incentivize municipal adoption...*), element Y must also be an infinitive phrase (*to penalize industrial emissions...*).

### Question 5
- **Correct Answer: C (*is*)**
  - **Indefinite Pronoun Rule**: In formal Standard English, `Neither` when used as the subject pronoun is singular (*"Neither of the [plural noun] is..."*). The intervening phrase *"of the two autonomous deep-sea research submersibles"* is a modifier. Singular present verb `is` is required.
  - **Distractors (A, B, D)**: Plural verbs (*are, were, have been*).

#### Question 6
- **Correct Answer: A (is)**
  - **Textual Proof**: The subject is singular "list"; the prepositional phrase "of ingredients" cannot change the verb.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **B (are)**: *Incorrect.* Agrees with nearby "ingredients" instead of "list."
  - **C (were)**: *Incorrect.* Past tense with no past-time signal.
  - **D (be)**: *Incorrect.* Bare form leaves the clause without a tensed verb.

#### Question 7
- **Correct Answer: D (reviews)**
  - **Textual Proof**: Time clauses after "after" use present tense for future meaning.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A (reviewed)**: *Incorrect.* Past tense places review before any timeline anchor.
  - **B (will review)**: *Incorrect.* "Will" never appears in the time clause itself.
  - **C (had reviewed)**: *Incorrect.* Past perfect with no earlier past event.

#### Question 8
- **Correct Answer: C (but also)**
  - **Textual Proof**: "Not only" pairs idiomatically with "but also."
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A (and)**: *Incorrect.* Breaks the correlative pair.
  - **B (but)**: *Incorrect.* Incomplete without "also."
  - **D (as well)**: *Incorrect.* Does not pair with "not only."

#### Question 9
- **Correct Answer: D (Maya)**
  - **Textual Proof**: Two women make "she" ambiguous; repeating the noun removes all doubt.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A (she)**: *Incorrect.* Could refer to either woman.
  - **B (they)**: *Incorrect.* Plural pronoun for one late person.
  - **C (it)**: *Incorrect.* Neuter pronoun for a person.

#### Question 10
- **Correct Answer: A (decided)**
  - **Textual Proof**: Past-tense finite verb completes the sentence.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **B (deciding)**: *Incorrect.* Participle leaves a fragment.
  - **C (decide)**: *Incorrect.* Base form mismatches singular past context.
  - **D (decision)**: *Incorrect.* Noun cannot serve as the verb.

#### Question 11
- **Correct Answer: C (those)**
  - **Textual Proof**: Plural "poems" requires plural "those."
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A (that)**: *Incorrect.* Singular for plural poems.
  - **B (them)**: *Incorrect.* Object pronoun cannot stand in comparison this way.
  - **D (deleting)**: *Incorrect.* "Than of Hafez" is ungrammatical.

#### Question 12
- **Correct Answer: D**
  - **Textual Proof**: "The family" are the ones who finished dinner — the doer follows the phrase.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A**: *Incorrect.* The dishes did not eat dinner.
  - **B**: *Incorrect.* "Going" names no dinner-eater.
  - **C**: *Incorrect.* The evening ate nothing.
