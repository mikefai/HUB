# Craft & Structure — Study Notes

Words in context, text purpose, and cross-text connections. Precision beats familiarity.

## Core Rules

- **Words in Context**: cover the choices, predict your own word from context clues (contrast words like *although*, cause words like *spurring*), then match. Connotation must fit the author's attitude.
- **Text purpose**: ask "what job does this passage do — report, argue, concede, illustrate?" Eliminate choices describing jobs the text never performs.
- **Cross-text**: map each author's claim separately first; then classify the relationship (agree / qualify / challenge / unrelated) before looking at options.
- **Trap radar**: *Verbatim Echo* (repeats passage words with wrong meaning), *Opposite Claim*, *Extreme Language* (*always, never, proves*), *Out of Scope*.
- **Hard-word toolkit**: break words into roots (*bene-* good, *mal-* bad, *con-* together, *-cide* kill); use the sentence's logic, not the word's fame.

## Worked Micro-Example

"Satellite data provide a far more _______ view spanning hundreds of miles" → predict "wide/broad" → **panoramic**. *Tentative* (uncertain) and *contentious* (controversial) mismatch the praising tone; *superficial* contradicts "networks spanning miles."

## Common Traps

- Picking a known word over the precise word.
- Choosing a true statement that doesn't answer the asked relationship.
- Ignoring contrast pivots (*however, although, yet*) that flip meaning.

## Self-Check (answers in the drills)

- Null results forced physicists to _______ assumptions. *(relinquish)*
- Biography avoids hagiography and doesn't _______ from exposing flaws. *(shrink)*
- Long-term effects remain distinctly _______. *(salutary)*
