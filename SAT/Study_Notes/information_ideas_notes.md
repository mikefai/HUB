# Information & Ideas — Study Notes

Central ideas, command of evidence, and inferences. Every answer must be traceable to the text or data.

## Core Rules

- **Central idea**: the choice the whole passage keeps returning to — too-narrow details and too-broad statements both fail.
- **Command of evidence**: match the claim's exact scope. A supporting finding must cover the same who/what/when as the claim.
- **Textual evidence**: quote-level alignment beats vibes. If no line supports it, eliminate it.
- **Quantitative evidence**: check axes, units, and denominators. A rising line supports growth; a single point supports nothing.
- **Inference**: must follow necessarily — if it *could* be false given the text, it's wrong. Never import outside knowledge.
- **Author attitude**: track adjectives and verbs (*quietly, tirelessly* = admiration; *claims without proof* = skepticism).

## Worked Micro-Example

Claim: bike-share cut downtown car trips. "Car entries to downtown dropped 18% in the launch year" supports it directly; "bikes are red" and "the mayor bikes" are irrelevant; "traffic fell everywhere for many reasons" weakens the causal link.

## Common Traps

- True-but-irrelevant findings.
- Extreme claims (*proves, solely, always*) on merely suggestive evidence.
- Reversed causality or wrong-group comparisons in data questions.

## Self-Check (answers in the drills)

- 60% first-timers, 80% under forty: conclusion? *(young, new audience)*
- Competing pesticide/mite studies: conclusion? *(evidence mixed, debated)*
- 46% vs 41% with ±3 error: race status? *(close)*
