# Advanced Math â€” Study Notes

Quadratics, polynomials, radicals, rationals, and exponentials. Most hard SAT Math points live here.

## Core Rules

- **Vertex form**: $f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k$ has vertex $(h, k)$. Expand to check against standard form.
- **Discriminant** $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$: $> 0$ two real roots, $= 0$ one (double) root, $< 0$ no real roots.
- **Vieta**: for $x^2 + bx + c = 0$ with roots $r_1, r_2$: sum $= -b$, product $= c$. Sum of squares $= (\text{sum})^2 - 2(\text{product})$.
- **Remainder theorem**: dividing $P(x)$ by $(x - a)$ leaves remainder $P(a)$. Factor theorem: $(x - a)$ is a factor exactly when $P(a) = 0$.
- **Radicals**: isolate the root, then square â€” and always verify, because squaring creates extraneous roots.
- **Rational exponents**: $x^{m/n} = (\sqrt[n]{x})^m$. Root first, then power.
- **Exponentials**: $P(t) = P_0 \cdot b^{t/k}$; $k$ is the doubling/halving period. Count periods, don't expand blindly.
- **Holes vs asymptotes**: a canceled factor gives a hole; a surviving denominator zero gives a vertical asymptote.

## Worked Micro-Examples

1. $4x^2 - 12x + c = 0$ has one solution $\Rightarrow 144 - 16c = 0 \Rightarrow c = 9$.
2. $\sqrt{2x + 4} = 6 \Rightarrow 2x + 4 = 36 \Rightarrow x = 16$ (verify: $\sqrt{36} = 6$ âœ“).
3. $27^{2/3} = (\sqrt[3]{27})^2 = 3^2 = 9$.

## Common Traps

- Accepting extraneous roots from squaring without checking.
- Confusing holes (canceled factor) with vertical asymptotes.
- Treating $x^{2/3}$ as $x \cdot \frac{2}{3}$.
