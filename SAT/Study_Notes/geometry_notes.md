# Geometry & Trigonometry — Study Notes

Circles, triangles, radians, and volume. Draw the figure, label everything, then pick the formula.

## Core Rules

- **Circle**: $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$. Complete the square to find center $(h, k)$ and radius $r$ (take the square root — $r^2 = 36$ means $r = 6$).
- **Radians**: $\text{arc} = r\theta$, $\text{sector} = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$, degrees $\times \frac{\pi}{180}$.
- **Special triangles**: 45-45-90 legs $1:1:\sqrt{2}$; 30-60-90 legs $1:\sqrt{3}:2$. Spot 5-12-13 and 6-8-10 triples.
- **Cofunction**: $\sin(A) = \cos(90^\circ - A)$. In a right triangle, $\sin(A) = \cos(B)$.
- **SOH-CAH-TOA**: sine = opposite/hypotenuse, cosine = adjacent/hypotenuse, tangent = opposite/adjacent.
- **Volume**: prism $lwh$; cylinder $\pi r^2 h$; cone $\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$; sphere $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$; 3D diagonal $\sqrt{l^2 + w^2 + h^2}$.
- **Similar figures**: lengths scale by $k$, areas by $k^2$. Perimeters scale like lengths.

## Worked Micro-Examples

1. $x^2 + y^2 - 8x + 10y + 5 = 0 \Rightarrow (x - 4)^2 + (y + 5)^2 = 36$: center $(4, -5)$, $r = 6$.
2. Arc $14\pi$, radius $21$: $\theta = 14\pi / 21 = 2\pi/3$.
3. Cone $r = 3$, $h = 12$: $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi(9)(12) = 36\pi$.

## Common Traps

- Reporting $r^2$ instead of $r$ after completing the square.
- Forgetting the $\frac{1}{3}$ in cone volume or the $\frac{1}{2}$ in sector area.
- Using the hypotenuse as a leg in area ($\frac{1}{2}ab$ needs the two legs).

## Self-Check (answers in the drills)

- Sector $r = 6$, $120^\circ$: area? *($12\pi$)*
- 30-60-90 with hypotenuse 10: long leg? *($5\sqrt{3}$)*
- Sphere $r = 3$: volume$/\pi$? *(36)*
