#### Question 6
- **Correct Answer: C (lavish)**
  - **Textual Proof**: Spending that "alarmed investors" fearing reserves "would run dry" is excessive; "lavish" means extravagantly rich.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A (frugal)**: *Incorrect.* Means thrifty — the opposite of alarming overspending.
  - **B (negligible)**: *Incorrect.* Means too small to matter; the sums threaten reserves.
  - **D (mandatory)**: *Incorrect.* Required spending would not alarm investors this way.

#### Question 7
- **Correct Answer: D (conciliatory)**
  - **Textual Proof**: Remarks that "eased tensions" by praising both sides aim to reconcile; "conciliatory" means intended to placate.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A (caustic)**: *Incorrect.* Biting remarks would inflame, not ease, tensions.
  - **B (perfunctory)**: *Incorrect.* Hasty, routine remarks would not resolve a summit crisis.
  - **C (ambiguous)**: *Incorrect.* Vagueness alone does not praise or propose timetables.

#### Question 8
- **Correct Answer: A (discern)**
  - **Textual Proof**: The sentence needs a verb meaning "perceive"; humans lack the receptors to detect the cue. "Discern" means to perceive.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **B (fabricate)**: *Incorrect.* Means to invent falsely; the issue is detection, not invention.
  - **C (relinquish)**: *Incorrect.* Means to give up; humans never had the ability.
  - **D (embellish)**: *Incorrect.* Means to decorate a story; wrong register entirely.

#### Question 9
- **Correct Answer: C (equivocal)**
  - **Textual Proof**: An ending that sustains years of debate about survival is open to multiple readings; "equivocal" means ambiguous.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A (didactic)**: *Incorrect.* Moralizing endings close debate rather than open it.
  - **B (chronological)**: *Incorrect.* Story order is unrelated to interpretive openness.
  - **D (verbose)**: *Incorrect.* Wordiness does not create deliberate ambiguity.

#### Question 10
- **Correct Answer: D (profligate)**
  - **Textual Proof**: Spending "lavishly" while bridges crumble is wasteful extravagance; "profligate" means recklessly wasteful.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A (austere)**: *Incorrect.* Severe simplicity — the opposite of lavish plazas.
  - **B (efficacious)**: *Incorrect.* Effective spending would fix bridges, not neglect them.
  - **C (parsimonious)**: *Incorrect.* Stinginess contradicts lavishness.

#### Question 11
- **Correct Answer: A (circumspect)**
  - **Textual Proof**: Refusing victory until replication is caution; "circumspect" means wary and careful.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **B (exuberant)**: *Incorrect.* Overflowing enthusiasm is the opposite of restraint.
  - **C (indifferent)**: *Incorrect.* She cares deeply — she demands replication.
  - **D (dogmatic)**: *Incorrect.* Rigid certainty contradicts her caution.

#### Question 12
- **Correct Answer: D (bombastic)**
  - **Textual Proof**: "Grand promises" with no workable plan is inflated empty rhetoric; "bombastic" means pompous and hollow.
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - **A (succinct)**: *Incorrect.* Brevity is the opposite of grand empty promises.
  - **B (pragmatic)**: *Incorrect.* Practical planning is exactly what is missing.
  - **C (candid)**: *Incorrect.* Frank honesty contradicts empty rhetoric.
